HIGH RISK RBI Circular May 22, 2026
Master Direction on KYC 2025 — Updates to UNSC 1267/1989 ISIL & Al-Qaida Sanctions List
Summary

The Reserve Bank of India has issued an update under the Master Direction on Know Your Customer (KYC), 2016, specifically addressing amendments to the United Nations Security Council's 1267/1989 Consolidated Sanctions List relating to ISIL (Da'esh) and Al-Qaida. This circular requires all regulated entities — including NBFCs, commercial banks, and urban co-operative banks — to immediately update their internal sanctions screening databases and remove the seven entries that have been delisted by the UNSC. The obligation arises under Section 51A of the Unlawful Activities Prevention Act, 1967, which mandates real-time compliance with UNSC sanctions lists. Entities must ensure their transaction monitoring and onboarding systems reflect these removals without delay, as continued blocking of these persons following delisting could itself constitute a compliance failure. The RBI circular references Chapter IX of the Master Direction, which governs international obligations and communications from global agencies. Compliance officers should verify that their sanctions list vendors or internal compliance teams have received and actioned the official UNSC communication. Given that NBFC-ICCs and NBFC-MFIs are explicitly named as addressees, this is a direct regulatory obligation rather than a general advisory. The action required is primarily operational — a database update — but needs to be logged, evidenced, and reported through normal compliance tracking mechanisms.

Applicability to Your Organisation
✓ Directly Applicable — NBFC-ICC / Digital Lending

Your organisation is registered as an NBFC-ICC with digital lending operations. The circular explicitly addresses NBFCs as a regulated entity class. Your KYC and onboarding workflows include sanctions screening, meaning the delisted entries must be removed from your active watchlist to avoid false positives in customer onboarding. No new obligations are created — this is an update to an existing compliance mechanism you already operate.

Conclusion

This circular is operationally straightforward but must be actioned promptly. The risk of non-compliance — even by omission — is reputational and regulatory. Since this is a sanctions list removal, failure to update creates the opposite problem: potentially blocking or flagging legitimate customers, which could trigger customer complaints or audit findings. No policy changes are required, only a database and screening system update.
